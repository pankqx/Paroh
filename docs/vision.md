# Vision

> Read this first. Read it again in six months. If a decision — technical, visual, or product — contradicts this document, the decision is wrong, not this document.

---

## Why Paroh Exists

Most journaling apps assume the hard part is remembering to write. It isn't. The hard part is that a blank page doesn't know what's wrong with you, and a to-do list doesn't care.

Paroh exists because healing from anxiety and depression is not an event, it's a practice — and practices need structure, not just space. A blank page is freedom for someone who already knows what they think. For someone whose mind is looping, a blank page is one more place to fail quietly.

Paroh is a structured place to think, feel, and slowly get better at both — disguised as a beautiful place to write.

Journaling is the medium. Becoming someone who can sit with their own mind without flinching is the point.

## The Problem We're Solving

Three things are broken today, and no single tool fixes all three:

**Journaling apps are empty rooms.** They give you a page and a date stamp and call it done. They don't know that today's entry should build on last Tuesday's breakthrough, or that the user has been avoiding the word "afraid" for three weeks.

**Habit and mood trackers are number factories.** They turn a person's inner life into a completion percentage. Streaks without meaning become their own source of anxiety — one more thing to fail at.

**Therapy is slow, expensive, and only available for one hour a week.** The other 167 hours, a person is alone with the exact thoughts therapy is trying to help them examine — and almost every method that works (thought records, exposure hierarchies, behavioral activation) is *homework*, done alone, on paper, between sessions. Nobody has built that homework a proper home.

Paroh is that home. A tool that holds structure the way a good therapist holds structure — gently, consistently, without ever making the user feel graded.

## Who We're Building For

Someone who already knows, intellectually, that their anxious thoughts aren't fully rational — and is exhausted by knowing that and still feeling them anyway.

Someone who has tried journaling before and stopped, because a blank page eventually feels like one more obligation.

Someone who is in therapy and needs a place to do the work between sessions — or someone who isn't in therapy yet, and needs a low-stakes place to start being honest with themselves before they're ready to be honest with another person.

We are not building for productivity enthusiasts collecting a second brain. We are not building Notion-for-feelings. If a feature makes Paroh better at organizing information and worse at helping someone feel less alone with their thoughts, it doesn't belong here.

## What We Believe

**Structure is kindness, not control.** A prompt is not a demand. It's a hand on the shoulder pointing at a door the user might not have noticed was open.

**Ownership is non-negotiable.** A person's private thoughts, written at their lowest and most honest, do not belong on someone else's server by default. They belong in a file, on their disk, in a format they can read without us.

**Small and true beats big and impressive.** One well-designed CBT prompt a person actually answers is worth more than a hundred features they never open.

**Consistency heals more than intensity.** Paroh is built for the person who shows up for four honest minutes every day, not the person who writes for three hours once a month. Every design decision should make the four-minute version easier, not the three-hour version more powerful.

**Beauty is not decoration, it's permission.** A person will not return to an ugly place to be vulnerable. The warmth in the interface is doing real clinical work — it's telling the user, before they've written a word, that this is a safe place to be unfinished.

**The tool should get out of the way of the feeling, then get back in the way of the avoidance.** Silent when the user is writing. Present when the user is stalling.

## What We Will Never Compromise

- We will never send a user's private entries anywhere without their explicit, specific, revocable consent.
- We will never gamify healing in a way that punishes a broken streak. A missed day is data, not a failure.
- We will never let an AI feature diagnose, prescribe, or claim to replace a licensed professional. It reflects. It never concludes.
- We will never lock a user's own words into a format they can't read without us. Plain Markdown, forever, is the floor — not a fallback mode.
- We will never add a feature that turns a private, honest tool into a performance surface by default. Publishing is opt-in, per entry, every time.
- We will never claim Paroh cures anything. We will claim, honestly, that consistent practice with the right structure measurably helps — because that's what's true, and the truth is enough.

## What Kind of Software We're Building

Paroh is closer to a well-made analog notebook than it is to a SaaS dashboard. It should feel considered the way Raycast feels considered, calm the way a good hotel room is calm, and honest the way a good doctor is honest — no false cheerfulness, no infantilizing, no dark patterns dressed up as "engagement."

It is local-first because privacy for this kind of content isn't a feature, it's the precondition for anyone telling the truth in it. It is file-based because a person's healing record should outlive our company, our servers, and any decision we make five years from now.

It should look, on first launch, like something a thoughtful person built for themselves and then decided to share — not like something a company built to capture a market.

## Where Paroh Is Going in Five Years

Paroh becomes the place someone opens before they open anything else — not because it demands attention, but because it's the one app that has never once made them feel worse for showing up.

It runs everywhere they do: the Ubuntu machine where it started, Windows, a phone in a waiting room, syncing through files they control rather than a service they rent.

It listens, eventually — not to replace the humans in a person's life, but to be there at 2am when none of those humans can be, asking the same kind of question a good therapist asks, in a voice that never judges and never pretends to be more than it is.

It has a small, quiet library of documented outcomes: not marketing claims, but honest data from people who used it consistently for six months and can point to what changed. If we're right about the design, that data will speak for itself. If we're wrong, we want to know before anyone else does.

## What Success Looks Like

A user who dreaded writing in January can't imagine skipping a day by July — not because of a streak counter, but because the four minutes have become the calmest part of their day.

A person in therapy brings their Paroh entries to a session and their therapist says the sessions have gotten better because of it.

Someone who was afraid to make eye contact with a barista writes, six months later, about a conversation they didn't rehearse in advance — and doesn't think to call it a big deal, because it isn't anymore.

## What Failure Looks Like

A user opens Paroh, sees a red notification badge and a broken streak, and closes the app feeling worse than before they opened it.

A feature roadmap that grows because it's easy to build things, not because each thing serves the four minutes.

A user's entries, written at their most honest, ending up somewhere they didn't explicitly choose to send them.

Paroh becoming impressive to build and forgettable to use.
